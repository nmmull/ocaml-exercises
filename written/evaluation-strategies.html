Evaluation Strategies

Recall the big-step call-by-value (CBV) semantics for the $λ$-calculus:

\[ \dfrac{}{\lambda x.e \Downarrow \lambda x.e}\ (\text{Fun}) \] \[ \dfrac{e_1 \Downarrow \lambda x.e \quad e_2 \Downarrow v_2 \quad (e' = [v_2/x]e) \quad e' \Downarrow v}{e_1\ e_2 \Downarrow v}\ (\text{AppCBV}) \]

and the big-step call-by-name (CBN) semantics for the $λ$-calculus:

\[ \dfrac{}{\lambda x.e \Downarrow \lambda x.e}\ (\text{Fun}) \] \[ \dfrac{e_1 \Downarrow \lambda x.e \quad (e' = [e_2/x]e) \quad e' \Downarrow v}{e_1\ e_2 \Downarrow v}\ (\text{AppCBN}) \]

Task 1

Give a closed1 expression \(e_0\) and a value \(v_0\) such that \(e_0 \Downarrow v_0\) is derivable using CBN semantics, but not derivable using CBV semantics. Your solution must include:

  1. the CBN semantic derivation of \(e_0 \Downarrow v_0\);
  2. a one sentence description for why \(e_0 \Downarrow v_0\) is not derivable using CBV semantics.

Hint: recall the expression \((\lambda x.\ x x)(\lambda x.\ x x)\).

Task 2

One notable feature of the semantic systems above is that they do not evaluate under binders. This is why we were able to ignore capture-avoidance when working with closed expressions. The full evaluation strategy (which does evaluate under binders — note the difference in the Fun rule) has the following semantic rules.

\[ \dfrac{x\ \text{is a variable}}{x \Downarrow x}\ (\text{Var}) \] \[ \dfrac{e \Downarrow v}{\lambda x.e \Downarrow \lambda x.v}\ (\text{Fun}) \] \[ \dfrac{e_1 \Downarrow \lambda x.e \quad e_2 \Downarrow v_2 \quad (e' = [v_2/x]e) \quad e' \Downarrow v}{e_1\ e_2 \Downarrow v}\ (\text{App}) \]

Determine a value \(v\) up to $α$-equivalence2 such that

\[ \lambda y.\ (\lambda x.\ \lambda y.\ x)\ y \Downarrow v \]

using the full evaluation strategy. Also provide the derivation. Hint: this will require $α$-renaming. You may choose any fresh variable you'd like.

Capture-Avoiding Substitution:

\begin{align*} [v / y]\ x &= \begin{cases} v & x = y \\ x & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\ [v / y]\ (\lambda x.\ e) &= \begin{cases} \lambda x.\ e & x = y \\ \lambda z.\ [v / y][z / x]e & x \in FV(v),\ z\ \text{is fresh} \\ \lambda x.\ [v / y]e & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\ [v / y]\ (e_1\ e_2) &= ([v / y]e_1)([v / y]e_2) \end{align*}

Footnotes:

1

An expression is closed if it has no free variables.

2

This means that your choice of \(v\) can differ from ours in the naming of bound variables.
